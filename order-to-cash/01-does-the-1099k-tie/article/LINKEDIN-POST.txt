The 1099-K says $39,907.56. The deposits say $38,492.25.
The return should say neither.

Every small-business client who takes cards gets a 1099-K from Square, Stripe or PayPal. It counts gross: before fees and refunds, with sales tax and tips inside. The bank only sees what's left. Books built from deposits come in below the 1099-K, and the IRS matching program notices.

So Agent 4 of One Painful Problem is a free tool for exactly that. Drop in the client's year of Square, Stripe or PayPal transactions and get:

→ What the 1099-K should say: box 1a, box 3, every month
→ The bridge to line 1: cash added, gift cards not counted twice, sales tax and tips as your call
→ The tie to the bank deposits, and the entry if the books booked deposits as sales
→ The questions for the client when it doesn't tie

Before release, a skeptical CPA review found 16 problems, including gift cards counted twice. All fixed. The 14 test cases and the full list are public.
