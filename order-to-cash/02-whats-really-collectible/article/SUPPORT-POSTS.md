## Post A: Thursday, November 19, 10:00 am Eastern (text only)

Two $500 payments. Same customer, same day, no check number.

My tool deleted one of them.

Before I release a tool, a separate AI reviewer goes through it as a skeptical CPA. In Agent 4,
the receivables review, it found 16 problems. This was the worst.

The tool looked for lines pasted into the report twice, and those two payments looked exactly
alike. So it kept one. The customer's balance went up $500, and it would have been chased for
money it had paid.

The fix: a line is only treated as a copy when the report's own total proves it. Otherwise both
stay, with a note to check them against the deposits.

Two more it caught:
→ An $800 check that paid a $500 and a $300 invoice disappeared from the clean-up entirely.
→ With no as-of date in the file, every invoice was "current". The page said nobody was overdue.

All fixed before release. Every one is now a test case, so it cannot come back.

## Post B: Tuesday, November 24, 10:00 am Eastern (one image: carousel slide 06)

A customer with a credit balance is not a receivable.

At year end, three things to do with every credit on a client's aging:

1. Find out why it is there. Paid twice? A payment posted to the wrong customer? A deposit?
2. Reclassify what is left to a liability on the balance sheet. Don't net it against receivables.
3. Never write it off to income. Old customer credits become unclaimed property after the state's dormancy period.

The free tool that finds these from a QuickBooks or Xero aging is in my last post.
