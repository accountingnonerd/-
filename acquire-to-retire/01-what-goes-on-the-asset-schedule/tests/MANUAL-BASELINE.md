# The same review by hand

What an accountant does today, for one client at year end, with the general ledger in Excel and last
year's depreciation schedule from the tax software. Minutes are estimates for a typical test file
(about 10 accounts, 200 ledger lines, 2 purchases recorded as assets, 3 hidden in expense accounts,
1 sale), not measurements.

| Step | By hand | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Export the general ledger; find the fixed asset, accumulated depreciation and depreciation accounts | 5 |
| 2 | Read every line of the fixed asset accounts: list purchases, spot credits (sales, returns), spot entries that were reversed | 10 |
| 3 | Filter repairs, supplies, small tools and computer expense for bills of $2,500 or more; add up bills split over several lines; set aside the service contracts | 20 |
| 4 | Roll cost and accumulated depreciation forward; tie the opening balances to last year's schedule | 10 |
| 5 | Compare depreciation booked with the tax software's figure; work out the entry | 5 |
| 6 | Write the list for the depreciation module, the entries and the questions for the client | 15 |
| | **Total** | **about 65 minutes** |

The tool does steps 1 to 5 in under a second and drafts step 6. What stays with the accountant: the
account check (step 2 on screen), the capitalize-or-expense decision for each bill, the client's
answers, and the method and life in the tax software.
