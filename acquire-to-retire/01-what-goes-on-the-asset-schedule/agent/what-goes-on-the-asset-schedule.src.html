Step 1 of 4: give the page the client's general ledger for the year.

What goes on the asset schedule?

At year end, this page reads the client's general ledger and lists what the depreciation schedule needs: equipment and vehicles bought this year, purchases hidden in repairs and supplies, assets sold or written off, and whether the books still agree with last year's schedule and this year's depreciation.

No file yet?

What file to ask the client for

One report: the general ledger for the client's whole year, every account, exported to Excel or CSV. Whoever keeps the books can export it in two minutes:

  • QuickBooks Online: Reports, General Ledger. Set the dates to the client's full year (for example 01/01/2026 to 12/31/2026), Accounting method Accrual, Run report, then Export, Export to Excel.
  • QuickBooks Desktop: Reports, Accountant & Taxes, General Ledger, the same dates, Accrual, then Excel, Create New Worksheet.
  • Xero: Reports, Account Transactions, the same dates, all accounts, Accrual, Update, then Export, Excel.
  • Other bookkeeping software: the report that lists every transaction by account (often called General Ledger, Account Transactions or Transaction Detail by Account), with the columns shown below.

Check before you send it:

  • It covers the client's own year, which may not be January to December.
  • It includes every account, even ones with no activity this year. If the report has an option to hide accounts with no activity or a zero balance, turn it off: the opening balances of the fixed asset accounts are needed.
  • One report on one sheet, with nothing deleted, sorted or retyped. The page checks that every account adds up to the report's own totals.
What the rows look like

One row per transaction, grouped under each account's name: a Date column, an Amount column (or Debit and Credit), and a Balance column. Each account starts with its beginning (opening) balance and ends with a total line.

What happens next

  1. Check the accounts (you check): the page sorts the accounts by name into fixed assets, accumulated depreciation, depreciation expense, and expense accounts where purchases hide. You correct any it got wrong.
  2. Decide what was found (you decide): the page lists this year's purchases, bills in expense accounts that may be equipment, and sales or write-offs. You choose capitalize or keep as expense, type the cost and depreciation of anything sold, and type the figures from last year's schedule and this year's depreciation.
  3. Print the workpaper (built from your choices): the list for the depreciation schedule, the adjusting entries and the questions for the client.

Where your data goes: nowhere. This page reads the file on this computer. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent.

The page finds; you decide. Whether to capitalize, how to depreciate and what to post are your judgment. The page does the reading and the arithmetic.
