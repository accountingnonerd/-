Step 1 of 4: give the page the client's general ledger for the year.

What goes on the asset schedule?

At year end, this page reads the client's general ledger and lists what the depreciation schedule needs: equipment and vehicles bought this year, purchases hidden in repairs and supplies, assets sold or written off, and whether the books still agree with last year's schedule and this year's depreciation.

No file yet?

What file to ask for

One report: the general ledger for the whole year, every account, exported to Excel or CSV. Whoever keeps the books can export it in two minutes:

  • QuickBooks Online: Reports, General Ledger, set the dates to the client's full year (for example 01/01/2026 to 12/31/2026), Run report, then Export, Export to Excel.
  • QuickBooks Desktop: Reports, Accountant & Taxes, General Ledger, the same dates, then Excel, Create New Worksheet.
  • Xero: Reports, Account Transactions, the same dates, all accounts, Update, then Export, Excel.

What it must look like: one row per transaction, grouped under each account's name, with a Date column, either an Amount column or Debit and Credit columns, and a Balance column. Each account starts with its beginning (opening) balance and ends with a total line. Do not delete or sort rows: the page checks that every account adds up.

What happens next

  1. Check the accounts (you): the page sorts the accounts by name into fixed assets, accumulated depreciation, depreciation expense, and expense accounts where purchases hide. You correct any it got wrong.
  2. Decide what was found (you): the page lists this year's purchases, possible purchases in expense accounts, and sales or write-offs. You tick what to capitalize and type the figures from last year's schedule and the tax software.
  3. Print the workpaper: the list for the depreciation schedule, the adjusting entries and the questions for the client.

Where your data goes: nowhere. This page reads the file on this computer. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent.

The page finds; you decide. Whether to capitalize, how to depreciate and what to post are your judgment. The page does the reading and the arithmetic.
