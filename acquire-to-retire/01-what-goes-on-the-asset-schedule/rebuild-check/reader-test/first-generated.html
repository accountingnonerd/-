Step 1 of 4: give the page the client's general ledger for the year.

    What goes on the asset schedule?

    Give this page a client's general ledger for the year, and it lists what the depreciation schedule and the year-end entries need: purchases recorded as fixed assets, bills in expense accounts that may be equipment, assets sold or written off, and whether the books agree with last year's schedule.

    No file at hand? Try a small made-up sample.

    What file to ask the client for

    The general ledger for the client's whole year, on the accrual basis, with every account, including those with no activity (turn off any option that hides them). One sheet. Nothing deleted, sorted or retyped.

    • QuickBooks Online: Reports, General Ledger, set the dates, Accounting method Accrual, Run report, Export to Excel.
    • QuickBooks Desktop: Reports, Accountant & Taxes, General Ledger, set the dates, Excel.
    • Xero: Reports, Account Transactions, set the dates, all accounts, Update, Export, Excel.
    • Other bookkeeping software: the report that lists every transaction by account, often called General Ledger, Account Transactions or Transaction Detail by Account, with the columns shown below.

    What happens next

    1. Check the accounts (you check): the page sorts the accounts; you confirm or correct them.
    2. Decide what was found (you decide): capitalize or keep as expense, type a sold asset's cost and depreciation, and type last year's totals.
    3. Print the workpaper (built from your choices).

    Where your data goes: nowhere

    The file is read by this page, on this computer. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent.
