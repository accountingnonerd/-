THE RULES (in this order)
Put every line of every account in one list, in file order, each with its account and role.
A. Fixed asset lines:
 1. Transfers: a credit in one fixed asset account and a debit of the same amount in another fixed asset account, with the same num (not blank) and the same date. Neither is an addition or a disposal. Pair each credit with the first unused debit that fits.
 2. Reversals: of the lines left, a credit and a debit of the same amount in the same account, same name (ignoring case), at most 31 days apart. Pair each credit with the first unused debit that fits. Neither counts.
 3. Payments: of the debits left, in one account, 3 or more with the same name (not blank) and the same amount: likely loan payments. They are not additions.
 4. Additions: the other debits. Flag: journal (type contains "journal"); trade-in (memo contains "trade", "allowance" or "net of"); land (its account's name contains the word "land" but not "improvement"; land is not depreciated).
 5. The other credits, each one of three kinds: "return" if the type contains "credit" or "refund" and not "journal" (it reduces a purchase's cost); "recorded" if it has a num and some line with the same num and date either debits an accumulated depreciation account or is in an account whose name contains gain, loss, sale or disposal (the sale was booked; check it); otherwise "proceeds" (money received for an asset was posted to the asset, so no gain or loss was recorded).
B. Bills in expense-to-review accounts:
 1. Group lines into bills: lines with the same date, type, num and name (across all review accounts) when num is not blank; when num is blank, lines with the same account, date, type and name. A bill's total is the sum of its lines; keep its accounts in order and its first line's position in the list.
 2. Bills with a negative total (credits) are applied first, oldest first (ties: earlier in the list):
    - If the credit has a num and some fixed asset account has a debit with the same num and date, it is a reclass: among the positive bills that are over the threshold and share an account with it, remove (set to zero) one whose total equals the credit exactly; if none, the first set of them (up to 14 bills, tried in order) whose totals add up exactly to it.
    - Otherwise, if it has a name: take the latest bill (positive, same name ignoring case, sharing an account, dated on or before it) and reduce its total by the credit.
 3. A bill whose total is now MORE than the threshold is a bill to decide (threshold $2,500.00 unless she types another). Order them by date, then position. Each starts undecided; she chooses Capitalize or Keep as expense. A bill "repeats" (likely a service contract) when its name has 3 or more bills to decide in the same first account and those bills are all the same amount, or their memos contain contract, maintenance, service, subscription, monthly, quarterly or annual. Repeating bills are still listed, with a note.
C. Possible sales elsewhere: credits in "other" accounts whose memo or name contains "sold", "sale of", "trade-in" (or "tradein") or "insurance proceeds", unless a "recorded" credit has the same num and date.
D. Depreciation booked = the sum of the lines in depreciation expense accounts. Cost opening = the sum of the fixed asset accounts' openings; accumulated depreciation opening = minus the sum of the accumulated depreciation accounts' openings (shown positive).
E. Mid-quarter: depreciable additions are the additions that are not land. The last three months start on the first day of the month two months before the period's last month (if the period is unknown: December 31 of the year of the latest line). If more than 40% of the depreciable additions (by amount) are dated on or after that day, flag it.
F. Figures she types (step 3d), each optional: last year's schedule total cost and total accumulated depreciation, and this year's depreciation. Opening differences = books' opening minus what she typed. Depreciation adjustment = typed this year's depreciation minus booked (positive: Dr Depreciation expense, Cr Accumulated depreciation; negative: the reverse). Something that is not a number shows "Not a number. Type digits, like 12,345.67." next to the box, and the old value is kept.
G. For each "proceeds" credit she can type the asset's original cost and its depreciation to date (from the schedule). Gain = money received - (cost - depreciation to date); a negative gain is a loss.
