Series 2 · Agent 1 | Why won't the bank rec tie?

Zac Lu, CPA, LSSBBFree, tested tools for accountants

The client sends three months of bank statements and a note: "QuickBooks says it's off. Can you look?"

The checking account at Mapleton Landscaping has 62 bank lines for the quarter and about a hundred entries in the books. The difference is not one round number you can search for. It is several things at once:

  • check #1033 entered as $683.50, paid by the bank as $638.50;
  • a $4,120.00 customer payment entered twice, two days apart;
  • three card sales recorded one by one that arrive as one deposit of $766.55;
  • three monthly service fees and a little interest the books never recorded;
  • a $415.00 check from last August that was never cashed.

Mapleton is invented. It is the sample client for this tool. But anyone who does catch-up bookkeeping or a quarterly close has met it. By hand, ticking off three statements and then comparing two lists line by line to find what is left takes us about two to three hours (an estimate, not a measurement).

This is Series 2, Agent 1: a free tool that does the matching, so you can spend the time on the decisions.

What it gives you

You drop in two files: the transactions downloaded from the bank's website, and the books' ledger for the same account from QuickBooks Online, QuickBooks Desktop or Xero. You get a reconciliation you can print or save as a PDF:

  • The proof. Balance per bank, plus deposits in transit, less outstanding checks; balance per books, plus or less the bank items and the corrections. For Mapleton both come to $109,509.83.
  • What is outstanding, and how old. Checks over six months are flagged stale-dated, over 90 days old, and deposits not at the bank after 10 days are flagged too.
  • What the bank has that the books do not, with a suggested treatment: a bank charge, interest, a returned check that goes back on the customer's balance, or a question for the client.
  • The entries and corrections, dated at the statement date.
  • The questions for the client. For Mapleton: did the payee of check #998 ever receive it?

The files are read on your computer. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent.

How it works: the page finds, you decide

1. Give the two files. Automatic. The page checks that each one adds up: the bank's running balance day by day, and the ledger against its own totals. If a line is missing, it stops and says so rather than reconcile a broken file.

2. Check what was read. You check. The statement dates, the beginning and ending balances, and which account in the books is the bank account. If the bank's download has no balances, you type them from the statement.

3. Decide what was found. You decide. The page matches checks by number first, then the same amount within a window (up to 60 days back, 180 for checks), then card batches (several book entries that add up to one deposit). What is left is where the time usually goes:

  • Book entries that look wrong. A check for a different amount than the bank paid, digits swapped, money in and out reversed, a voided check the bank paid anyway, a check paid twice, an entry made twice. The page decides none of them. Until you do, both sides count as separate items, and the reconciliation says it is not finished.
  • At the bank, not in the books. Each with a suggested treatment you can change.
  • Outstanding. With its age. A stale check waits for you: keep it outstanding, or reverse it to a liability.

It also does the part that is easy to forget: which checks were already outstanding when the bank's file starts. It works that out from the two starting balances. When more than one set of old checks would fit, it asks you to tick the right ones instead of choosing.

4. Print the reconciliation. Built from your choices. Anything still open is listed at the top in red.

What the reviews found

Before release, a separate AI review played a skeptical CPA and ran 18 file pairs of its own. Its verdict on the first version: not ready. The worst problem was that the start of the bank file worked like a plug. Whatever old items added up to the difference were declared outstanding, so the rec tied by construction. In one of its files, an unrecorded $1,200 loan payment disappeared that way. It also found a customer's check number matched to the company's own check, and "fee" matched inside "coffee". All of these are fixed, and each is now a test file. The tool must pass 28 test files, 839 checks in all.

A second review played a first-time user working from the screen alone. It found that the page could say "It ties" while an unexplained difference sat in the opening balances. Now it says "ties" only when every entry that looks wrong is decided and the start is explained. A stale check still to decide is named next to it.

The last check was the prompt itself. A fresh AI session, given only the prompt, rebuilt the tool and ran it through the same tests: 22 of 27 on the first try, all 27 after one round of fixes taken from the prompt. It listed every place the prompt was unclear, and those are now spelled out. It also found a real bug: a statement balance typed wrong stopped the page with no way back except starting over. That is fixed, and it is test file 28.

Build it yourself

You don't need to download anything from me to have this tool. It comes from one prompt, and you can rebuild it with an AI assistant in about 15 minutes. You need no coding and no Excel skills.

What you need

  • A computer with a web browser and an internet connection. The page you build loads its spreadsheet reader from the internet each time it opens. (The ready-made tool in the repository works offline.)
  • An AI chat assistant that can write a long piece of code in one reply, such as Claude at claude.ai. The reply is long (about 1,000 lines), so a free plan may stop partway; a paid plan is safer.
  • About 15 minutes, most of it waiting for the reply.

Steps

  1. Copy the prompt. The safest copy is the master on GitHub. The repository's name is a single hyphen (github.com/accountingnonerd/-), and that is right. Open PROMPT.txt and press the "Copy raw file" button (two small squares next to "Raw", top right of the file). The prompt is long, about 34,000 characters; some assistants show a paste that long as an attached file. That is normal. It is also printed in full below.
  2. Paste it into a new chat in your AI assistant and send it.
  3. Wait for one long block of code. If the assistant offers it as a file to download, or shows it in a side panel with a download or copy button, use that. If the reply stops partway, write: "Please send the complete file again in one code block."
  4. Save it as a web page. If there was no download, copy the whole code block into a plain-text editor and save it as why-wont-the-bank-rec-tie.html. On Windows, use Notepad: in the Save dialog, set "Save as type" to "All files", or it saves as .html.txt. On a Mac, use TextEdit: first choose Format, then Make Plain Text, and when it asks, choose "Use .html".
  5. Open it. Double-click the file. You should see "Why won't the bank rec tie?" and two boxes to drop files into.
  6. Try it on the sample client before any real file. Download the Mapleton bank download and the Mapleton ledger. On each page, press "Download raw file". Drop the bank file in the box "The bank's download" and the ledger in "The books' ledger", and follow the steps on the screen.

In step 2 the balances are $81,868.88 and $108,730.18; press "This is right". In step 3a, choose "Books wrong: correct them" for check #1033 and "Entered twice: remove one" for the Birch Dental deposit. The top line already reads "It ties ... If it does not work

  • A blank page, or half a page: the code was cut off. Ask the assistant for the complete file again.
  • Nothing happens when you drop a file: the page could not load its spreadsheet reader. Check your internet connection; a company firewall can block it. Or use the ready-made tool.
  • Different numbers: tell the assistant what you expected, quoting the line above, and ask it to fix the file so it follows the prompt exactly.
  • Still stuck: the ready-made, tested tool is in the repository. It is one file that works offline.

The prompt. Copy it from the master copy on GitHub if you can. LinkedIn can change quotes and line breaks.

Build me a complete, working tool as ONE self-contained HTML file named why-wont-the-bank-rec-tie.html. Reply with the whole file in a single code block, with nothing left out or shortened, so I can save it and open it in my browser.

WHO IT IS FOR AND WHAT IT DOES
It is for a US accountant reconciling a client's bank account. She is expert in accounting, not technical, and works on a desktop. She gives it two files: the transactions downloaded from the bank's website for the months to reconcile, and the books' ledger detail for the same account (exported to Excel or CSV from QuickBooks Online, QuickBooks Desktop or Xero), starting a few months before the bank's first date. The page matches the bank's lines to the books, lists what is outstanding and how old it is, what the bank has that the books do not, the book entries that look wrong (a check for the wrong amount, digits swapped, a sign reversed, an entry made twice), and the reconciliation that proves it. The page finds; she decides. Everything runs in the browser; nothing is uploaded. All money is whole cents (integers), money in positive, worked out in code.

TECHNICAL BASICS
- Load the spreadsheet reader with: <script src="https://cdnjs.cloudflare.com/ajax/libs/xlsx/0.18.5/xlsx.full.min.js"></script>
- Read each file with XLSX.read: .csv or .txt as text (decode the bytes as UTF-8) with {type:"string", raw:true}; anything else as {type:"array"}. Use the sheet with the most rows, via XLSX.utils.sheet_to_json(sheet, {header:1, raw:true, defval:null, blankrows:true}).
- Refuse a .pdf at once ("... is a PDF. Download or export it as CSV or Excel instead."), and any extension other than xlsx, xlsm, xls, csv, txt ("... is not an Excel or CSV file.").
- Set window.__report to the report object (listed at the end) every time the reconciliation is produced or redone, without fields whose names start with "_". Set it to undefined when a new file starts loading. If anything throws, show the error and set window.__report = {status:"ERROR", message}.
- Large type (18px or more), plain words, high contrast, light background. Works on a phone: on narrow screens every table becomes stacked cards, each cell labelled with its column name. Print styles: see step 4.

READING NUMBERS AND DATES
- An amount is a number (cents = round(n*100)) or text. Text: change the true minus sign (U+2212) to "-"; blank, "-", en dash or em dash is no amount. Remove a leading "USD" or "US$". A trailing "CR" or "DR" (any case, after a space or not) is removed; DR makes it negative. Parentheses make it negative; a trailing minus makes it negative; then $, commas and spaces are removed; a leading minus makes it negative. What is left must be digits with an optional decimal point; anything else is unreadable (not the same as no amount).
- A date becomes a day number. Accept: an Excel serial number between 20000 and 80000 (day = n - 25569); YYYY-MM-DD; M/D/YYYY or M/D/YY (US order, two-digit years are 20YY); "15 Mar 2026" or "15-Mar-2026"; "March 15, 2026" or "Mar. 15 2026". A date that does not exist (month 13, February 30, 31/12/2026) is not a date.
- Show dates on screen as M/D/YYYY and money as $1,234.56 (negative "-$1,234.56"). In window.__report, every date is "YYYY-MM-DD" and money is dollars as a number.

READING THE BANK'S DOWNLOAD (box 1)
1. Heading row: the first of the first 15 rows whose cells (lower case, trimmed) include a date heading (date, posting date, post date, posted date, transaction date, effective date) and either "amount" or both a debit heading (debit, debits, withdrawal, withdrawals, withdrawal amount, payments, money out, debit amount) and a credit heading (credit, credits, deposit, deposits, deposit amount, money in, credit amount). For each column take the first heading in each list that is present: description (description, transaction, transaction description, memo, payee, name, details; if it is "details" and there is also "description", use "description"); check number (check or slip #, check number, check no., check no, check #, chk #, check, serial number); balance (balance, running bal., running balance, ledger balance, available balance); status (status, transaction status).
2. Title lines are the rows above the heading row (or the first 12 rows if there is none), each row's non-empty cells joined by spaces. If the title lines say general ledger, account transactions, transaction detail, trial balance, profit and loss (or profit & loss) or balance sheet, or the heading row contains num, split, transaction type, memo/description, contact or source: it is the books' ledger in the wrong box. Status NOT_RECOGNIZED, message "This looks like the books' ledger, not the bank's download. Put it in box 2, and the bank's own download in box 1.", and report swapped: true.
3. A summary block above the heading row: a row containing "beginning balance" with an amount gives the beginning balance (the last amount in the row); a row containing "ending balance" gives the ending balance, and if the row (its cells joined by spaces) says "as of" followed by a date, the statement end date.
4. No heading row: if the first row's first cell is a date and its second cell is an amount, read it as headerless: date in column 1, amount in column 2, check number in column 4, and the description is the last non-empty cell from column 3 on that is not "*" (skipping column 4 when it is all digits). Otherwise: status NOT_RECOGNIZED, "This is not a bank download I can read. I need the transactions downloaded from the bank's website: a date, a description and an amount (or withdrawals and deposits) on each line."
5. Each row below: skip blank rows. If the date cell is not a date, skip it, but if it has a digit in it, the row is unread (keep its row number). If the status says "pending", leave it out and count it. The amount: the amount column; or with two columns, a non-zero debit is money out (negative), else a non-zero credit is money in. An unreadable amount makes the row unread. A row with no amount: if its description says "beginning balance" and it has a balance, that is the beginning balance (used only if the summary has none); otherwise skip it. Keep each line's row, date, description, check number (the cell, only if 1 to 8 digits), amount and balance. No lines: NOT_RECOGNIZED as in 4.
6. If the first line's date is after the last line's, the file is newest first: reverse the lines (layout gets ", newest first"). Layout text: "date, description, amount", "withdrawals and deposits columns" or "no heading row".
7. The running balance, when every line has one, is checked day by day (banks list a day's lines in any order): going day by day, the balance after each day's lines must be one of that day's balances. If no beginning balance is known, try each balance of the first day minus that day's total (the last line's first) and take the first that gets through every day (else the first tried). The first day where it fails is the gap (keep that day's first row; only the first gap is reported); carry on from that day's last balance. The ending balance, if not in the summary, is the running total at the end. Balances disagree when the file gives both a beginning and an ending, there is no gap, and beginning + all lines is not the ending; or when she typed both and beginning + all lines is not what she typed. (One typed and one from the file is not checked.)

READING THE BOOKS' LEDGER (box 2)
1. Heading row: the first of the first 20 rows whose cells (lower case, trimmed) include "date", and "amount" (or both "debit" and "credit"), and "balance" or "running balance". Columns: date; type = "transaction type", else "type", else "source"; num = "num", else "reference", else "ref", else "no."; name = "name", else "contact"; memo = "memo/description", else "memo", else "description"; amount; debit; credit; balance = "balance", else "running balance". Layout: Xero if there is "running balance"; else QuickBooks Online if there is "amount"; else QuickBooks Desktop.
2. Title lines as for the bank. If there is no heading row: status NOT_RECOGNIZED: "This is a trial balance, not a general ledger. I need the books' ledger for the bank account: every transaction, grouped by account, with a Date column, an Amount column (or Debit and Credit), and a Balance column." (naming a trial balance, a profit and loss (or income statement), a balance sheet or an aging report when the title lines say so; otherwise "This is not a general ledger I can read.").
3. Rows below the heading row, in order (skip blank rows). Keep a stack of open accounts:
   - A row with a cell "Beginning Balance" or "Opening Balance": the account on top of the stack gets its opening balance from the balance column.
   - A row with a cell "Closing Balance": the account closed last gets its closing balance from the balance column.
   - A row whose first non-empty cell starts with "Total" (any case): if it is exactly "Total", ignore it. Otherwise it closes the account on top of the stack (pop it): store its total (the amount column, or debit minus credit) and, if the balance cell has an amount, its total balance.
   - A row with a readable date: a line of the account on top of the stack, with row, date, type, num, name, memo and amount (amount column, or debit minus credit). If the balance cell has an amount, keep it as the account's last balance. If no account is open, the row is unread.
   - A row with an amount but no readable date: unread.
   - Any other row: an account header. Its label is the first non-empty cell, cleaned: remove a trailing code in parentheses of up to 10 letters, digits, dots or hyphens; remove a leading account number like "10100 · " or "1010 " or "1010 - ". Its opening is the balance cell if it has an amount, else 0. If an account is open on the stack, the new one is its sub-account "Parent:Child". Push it.
   - Every account's ending = opening + its lines. Keep only accounts with lines or a non-zero opening.
4. The file adds up: for each account header read, together with all its sub-accounts, its total must be the sum of all their lines, its total balance the sum of their endings, and its own closing balance and last balance its own ending. List those that do not.
5. Company: the first title line that does not contain "general ledger", "account transactions", "transaction detail", "basis", "period" or a four-digit year.

WHICH ACCOUNT
The bank account is the one she chooses in step 2; until then, guess: among accounts that are not sub-accounts and whose names do not contain undeposited, petty or clearing, the first whose name contains "checking", else "operating", else "bank", else savings, money market or cash; else the first account. Its sub-accounts ("Checking:Payroll") are part of it: their openings and lines are included. If the chosen name contains credit card, the word card, visa, mastercard, amex, american express, discover, the word loan, or line of credit: stop with CARD_NOT_SUPPORTED (below) and show only that message and the account box.
The books' lines: all lines of the account and its sub-accounts, sorted by date, then row. Opening = the sum of their openings.

THE RECONCILIATION (in this order)
All bank lines count, whatever the end date. Number the bank lines 0, 1, 2 ... in file order (after reversing) and the books' lines 0, 1, 2 ... in sorted order; these numbers are the "i" in the ids below. Start = the earliest bank date. End (the statement date) = the date she typed, else the summary's "as of" date, else the latest bank date. Bank beginning and ending balances: what she typed, else the file's; if only the beginning is known, ending = beginning + all lines. Books' lines dated after the end are left out ("after the statement"). Of the rest, lines of zero are voided entries: kept aside. Every rule below works through the bank lines by date, then number, and when several book lines fit takes the one nearest in days (ties: lower number). Days apart = book date minus bank date. A check number on a bank line: its check number cell, else "check", "chk" or "ck" followed by an optional "#" and 3 to 6 digits in the description; leading zeros are ignored. A book check is a line whose num is 3 to 6 digits and whose amount is negative.
1. Checks by number. For each bank line of money out with a check number: book lines (not yet used) of money out with the same num. One with the same amount: matched. Otherwise, if there is one: a near miss "check amount differs" with the first of them. Otherwise, a voided book line with that num: near miss "voided but paid". Otherwise, if that check number was already matched or paired: near miss "paid twice".
2. Same amount. For each bank line not yet matched or paired: unused book lines of exactly the same amount, from 60 days before the bank line (180 for book checks) to 5 days after. A book line that is not a check and is dated more than 10 days before the start cannot match. A bank line with a check number cannot match a book check with a different number.
3. Several book lines to one bank line (card batches, deposits of several checks). For each bank line still open: unused book lines of the same sign from 5 days before to 1 day after it; keep the 12 nearest, put them in date order, and look for 2 to 6 of them that add up to it exactly, trying sets in binary-counter order (number them 0, 1, 2 ...; for k = 1, 2, 3 ... set k holds item j when bit j of k is 1). The first set found is matched.
4. One book line to several bank lines. For each book line still unused, by date: open bank lines of the same sign dated from 1 day before to 5 days after it; the 12 nearest, in date order; 2 to 6 that add up to it, as in 3.
5. Near misses, for each bank line still open: first, an unused book line within 10 days, same sign, different amount, whose difference divides by 9 and which has the same digits in another order: "transposed". Else an unused book line of exactly the opposite amount within 10 days: "sign".
6. Entered twice: an unused book line with a name, dated on or after the start, with a book line matched in 1 to 4 (also on or after the start) of the same amount and name (any case) within 5 days: "entered twice" (once at the bank).
7. Before the bank's dates. Books' balance the day before = opening + book lines (not zero) before the start. Of the book lines before the start, the unused ones in date order are the old items; the candidates are the old book checks and the old items dated 10 days or fewer before the start. R = books' balance the day before - bank beginning balance - the old book lines that were matched or paired in 1 to 5. What is still outstanding from before:
   Test in this order. No beginning balance: mode "unknown", and R, what is left and the candidates are null or empty. Otherwise, if there are candidates:
   - R = 0: mode "none";
   - candidates add up to R all together: mode "all", all of them;
   - 20 candidates or fewer: look for sets adding up to R (binary-counter order, stop at 2): one set: mode "unique", that set; two: mode "ambiguous"; none: "not explained";
   - more than 20: mode "too many";
   - ambiguous or too many: she ticks the ones still outstanding (ids "k" + i); only those count.
   If there are no candidates: R = 0 is mode "none"; otherwise (the books' file starts on the bank's first date) look among the open bank lines dated up to 45 days after the start (the first 20 by date) for the first set adding up to R. Found: mode "bank lines", those are suggested; she ticks each one recorded in the books before the file starts (ids "b" + i). A ticked line is neither bank-only nor outstanding. Not found: "not explained".
   Left at the start = R - the old items counted as still outstanding - the ticked bank lines.
8. Her decisions (step 3a), each near miss with id "n" + its position in the list (in the order found) and each entered-twice with "u" + its position. Each starts "undecided". Choices: "fix" (the books are wrong), "bank" (a bank error), "not" (not the same). Until decided, or when "not": the bank line is bank-only (with a treatment in 3b like any other) and the book line, if there is one, is outstanding ("voided but paid" and "paid twice" have none). "fix": the book corrections change by bank amount - book amount (for "voided but paid", the bank amount; for entered twice, minus the entry's amount). "bank": the bank corrections change by book amount - bank amount (a check paid twice: minus the bank amount).
9. Outstanding at the end: unused book lines dated on or after the start, plus the old items still outstanding (7), plus the undecided or "not" near misses' and entries' book lines. Bank-only: the bank lines not matched, split, paired or ticked, plus the undecided or "not" near misses' bank lines. Both sorted by date, then number. Each outstanding item's age = end - its date, in days. A book check over 180 days is stale (UCC 4-404); over 90, old. Anything outstanding that is not a book check, over 10 days old, is a late deposit (money in) or late payment. A book check under 90 days has no flag. A stale check waits for her decision, id "o" + i: "keep" or "reverse" (reverse to a liability). A reversed stale check stays listed with its decision and leaves the outstanding total; the book corrections go up by the check's size (book corrections = book corrections - its negative amount), because the reversing entry puts it back in the books.
10. The proof. Books' balance at the end = opening + all the account's lines up to the end. Adjusted bank = bank ending balance + outstanding (money in adds, money out subtracts) + bank corrections. Adjusted books = books' balance at the end + bank-only + book corrections. Difference = adjusted bank - adjusted books. It ties when the difference is 0, nothing in step 3a is undecided, and nothing is left at the start. A stale check not yet decided does not stop the tie; it is listed as still to decide.
11. Kinds of bank-only lines, from the description (any case): interest if money in, the word interest, and not "charge"; returned if NSF (as a word), "returned item", "return item" or "chargeback" and not the word fee; fee if money out and service charge, monthly fee, monthly service fee, monthly maintenance fee, maintenance fee, account fee, wire fee, wire transfer fee, overdraft, analysis fee, atm fee, or the word fee; otherwise other. Its suggested treatment (step 3b, she can change it, ids "b" + i): "later" if a book line after the statement has the same amount within 30 days of it ("Already in the books after the statement date"), else fee "Bank service charge", interest "Interest income", returned "Returned item: back on the customer's balance", other "Ask the client what it was".

MESSAGES (code, level), only when they apply. Level "stop" shows in a red box at the top, each starting "Stop."; "care" and "info" in a list "Check these first", care first, labelled "Decide." and "For your file.". Each names the step where she acts. When the bank's download has unread rows, gaps, the books do not add up, or the balances disagree, show only the stop messages and nothing below "Check these first"; except when the only stop is BALANCES_DISAGREE from balances she typed: then also show step 2, so she can correct them.
- NOT_RECOGNIZED (stop): status NOT_RECOGNIZED and only this message, with the texts above; shown in the start screen's problem box. When the books' ledger is in box 1, the box has a button id="swap" "Swap the two files".
- UNREAD_ROWS (stop): "I could not read the date or the amount on N lines of the bank's download (row 4, ...). Rather than leave them out, I stop: download the file again, or fix the format of those lines."
- BANK_FILE_GAPS (stop): the running balance jumps at row N: a line is missing or was changed; download it again and do not edit it.
- BOOKS_DO_NOT_ADD_UP (stop): names the accounts; export the ledger again and do not edit it.
- BALANCES_DISAGREE (stop): beginning balance plus the lines is not the ending balance (from the file, or both typed); gives both figures.
- CARD_NOT_SUPPORTED (stop): "... looks like a credit card or a loan. This version reconciles bank accounts (checking, savings, money market); cards and loans are on the list for later. If it is a bank account, choose it again in step 2."
- ACCOUNT_CHOSEN (info): the books' file has other accounts; which one was reconciled; choose another in step 2.
- SUBACCOUNTS (info): the sub-accounts are part of the bank account and are included.
- PENDING_LEFT_OUT (info): N pending bank lines left out: not yet posted.
- NEED_BALANCES (care): the download has no balances; type the statement's beginning and ending balances in step 2.
- DOES_NOT_TIE (care): the adjusted bank balance is $X more or less than the adjusted books.
- START_NOT_EXPLAINED (care): gives the books' balance the day before, the bank's beginning balance and R; check the bank's beginning balance, start the books' file earlier, or look for an old entry that was changed.
- START_AMBIGUOUS (care): checks from before the start should add up to R and more than one set does (or there are too many); tick the ones still outstanding in step 3c.
- START_BANK_LINES (care): the books' file starts on the bank's first date; N early bank lines add up to R; tick them in step 3b if recorded before; better, export the books from earlier.
- CHECK_AMOUNT_DIFFERS, VOIDED_BUT_PAID, PAID_TWICE, TRANSPOSED, SIGN_ERROR, BOOK_DUPLICATES (care): one for each kind found; decide in step 3a.
- BANK_ONLY (care): N bank items are not in the books ($total); choose how to treat each in step 3b.
- RETURNED_ITEMS (care): a returned (NSF) item: put it back on the customer's balance and ask them to pay again; not a bank charge.
- STALE_CHECKS (care): checks outstanding more than six months are stale-dated (UCC 4-404); ask the payee; an uncashed check becomes unclaimed property after the state's dormancy period: reverse it to a liability, never to income; decide each in step 3c.
- OLD_OUTSTANDING (info): checks over 90 days: worth a call to the payee.
- LATE_ITEMS (care): deposits or electronic payments not at the bank after 10 days: probably never made, entered twice, or wrong date or amount.
- BATCHES (info), SPLITS (info), AFTER_STATEMENT (info): what was matched several to one, one to several, and the book entries after the statement date left out.
Status: NOT_RECOGNIZED as above, otherwise COMPUTED.

What it doesn't do

  • It reconciles bank accounts only. Credit cards and loans are refused with a message, for now.
  • It makes one reconciliation for the whole span. For a quarter's catch-up, that is one rec at the quarter end, not one per month.
  • It does not read last month's outstanding list. When the starting balances leave more than one answer, it asks you to tick the items from that list.
  • It does not post anything. It proposes entries and corrections; you decide.
  • It has not met real client exports yet. It was tested on files built from the banks' and the products' published layouts. If your download doesn't read, tell me and I will add the layout.
